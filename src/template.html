    Further afield

    The mountain, the wetlands and the big day trips of the southern Berg.

    Around townSeasonal

    The open gardens

    Private gardens along the lane open to visitors for the Underberg Open Gardens weekend each November, with proceeds going to local charities. Outside open days, enjoy them from the road.

    Around townAny time

    Hlogoma views

    Underberg means "under the mountain", and the mountain is Hlogoma, the "place of echoes". The lane gives open views of it across the farmland, best in low morning or evening light.

    Around townBring binoculars

    Wetlands and cranes

    The highland wetlands around Underberg are home to grey crowned, blue and wattled cranes. Keep a respectful distance and stay in your car near nesting areas.

    Photo: Charles J. Sharp · CC BY-SA 4.0

    Nearby~5 km

    Himeville Museum

    Underberg's neighbouring village keeps its local history in the old fort and gaol, built at the turn of the 1900s. Displays cover San heritage, early settlers and farm life.

    Day tripPassport needed

    Sani Pass

    The famous switchback road climbs the escarpment to the Lesotho border at 2,876 m. Go with a 4x4 tour operator and take your passport; the summit lodge serves a hot lunch with a view.

    Photo: Vaiz Ha · CC BY 2.0

    Day tripHiking

    Garden Castle and Rhino Peak

    A southern gateway into the Drakensberg park, with trout streams, short valley walks and the long, steep day hike up to Rhino Peak. Book permits at the reserve gate.

    Flora and fauna

    What to look out for in the grassland, wetlands and cliffs around the lane. Bring binoculars and keep your distance from nesting birds.

    Fauna

    • Bearded vulture Gypaetus barbatus

      One of Africa’s rarest raptors. Southern Africa’s only population lives in these mountains, gliding along the escarpment.

      Cliffs · all year

    • Cape vulture Gyps coprotheres

      Breeds in colonies on the high sandstone cliffs and rides the thermals over the foothills.

      Cliffs · all year

    • Wattled crane Bugeranus carunculatus

      South Africa’s rarest crane. The highland wetlands of this district are among its last strongholds.

      Wetlands · all year

    • Southern bald ibis Geronticus calvus

      A glossy ibis with a red crown, feeding in short, freshly burnt grassland.

      Grassland · winter and spring

    • Eland Taurotragus oryx

      Africa’s largest antelope, and the animal the San painted most often on the rock shelters of the Berg.

      Foothills · all year

    • Grey rhebok Pelea capreolus

      A woolly-coated antelope of the high grassland, usually seen in small groups on open slopes.

      High grassland · all year

    Flora

    • Common sugarbush Protea caffra

      Gnarled protea trees scattered across the rocky hillsides, with pale pink flower heads that draw sugarbirds.

      Rocky slopes · summer

    • Red-hot poker Kniphofia

      Flame-coloured spikes along streams and on damp slopes.

      Streams and seeps · summer

    • Agapanthus Agapanthus campanulatus

      Round blue heads on the grassland slopes, and a staple of the gardens along the lane.

      Grassland · summer

    • Fire lily Cyrtanthus

      Bright red trumpets that push up through the blackened ground soon after a veld burn.

      Burnt veld · spring

    • Everlastings Helichrysum

      Papery silver and gold flowers. The Drakensberg has dozens of species.

      Grassland · summer and autumn

    • Tree fern Cyathea dregei

      A sturdy grassland tree fern that survives fire, found along stream banks and in moist gullies.

      Stream banks · all year

    Under the mountain

    People have lived in these foothills for thousands of years. The village itself is young, built around a railway line and the farms it served.

    "Hlogoma" is usually translated as the place of echoes.
    1. THOUSANDS OF YEARS AGO

      San hunter-gatherers

      The San painted the rock shelters of the Drakensberg, leaving one of the richest bodies of rock art in Africa. Many sites are protected and can be visited only with a guide.

    2. 1800s

      Farms in the foothills

      Settler families took up farms in the high grasslands below the escarpment, raising cattle and sheep on the summer grazing.

    3. 1917

      The railway and the village

      A branch line from Pietermaritzburg reached the area, and Underberg grew up around its terminus as the trading centre for the district.

    4. 1948

      A vehicle tops Sani Pass

      The old bridle path used by pack mules into Lesotho was first driven by a Land Rover, starting its life as one of the country's best-known mountain roads.

    5. 2000

      World Heritage listing

      The uKhahlamba-Drakensberg Park was listed by UNESCO for its scenery and rock art, later extended across the border as the Maloti-Drakensberg Park.

    6. TODAY

      Loopy Lane opens its gates

      Gardeners on the lane open their gardens to visitors during the November Open Gardens weekend, raising money for local charities.

    Four seasons on the lane

    Underberg sits high enough for frost in winter and green, stormy afternoons in summer. Every season looks different from the road.

    NOVEMBER – FEBRUARY

    Green and growing

    • Lush grassland and wildflowers along the verges
    • Open Gardens weekend in November
    • Warm mornings, with thunderstorms most afternoons
    MARCH – MAY

    Golden and clear

    • Settled weather and long views to the escarpment
    • Grass turns gold and the poplars and willows colour up
    • Good walking and fishing weather

    Photo: Graham Maclachlan · CC BY-SA 3.0

    JUNE – AUGUST

    Frost and snow on the Berg

    • Crisp, sunny days and hard frosts at night
    • Snow on the peaks after cold fronts; Sani Pass can close
    • Fireside evenings and the clearest skies of the year

    Photo: GrantsJ1 · CC BY-SA 4.0

    SEPTEMBER – OCTOBER

    Fresh green after the burn

    • New grass pushes through the winter firebreaks
    • Blossom in the gardens and birds returning
    • Windy days, with the first rains arriving

    Photo: Peter Warren · CC0
